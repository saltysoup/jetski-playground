Architectural Findings & Why You Need Lustre KV Offloading & LLM-D

  • Why 16 × B200 GPUs Outperform 32 × H100 GPUs by +179.3% (2.79× Faster) on Long-Context 32k/1k: Despite having half the total GPU count (16 vs. 32), B200 delivers 170.1 tok/s output throughput at saturation compared to 60.9 tok/s on H100. This is driven by Blackwell's FP4/FP8 compute density and 2 nodes over hardware-offloaded 800 Gbps RoCEv2 RDMA versus 4 Hopper nodes communicating over GPUDirect TCPXO Ethernet AllReduce.
  • Proof of the Pareto Saturation Wall:
    • On B200: Output throughput plateaus at ~167.7 — 170.1 tok/s from c = 8 to c = 512. Meanwhile, Inter-Token Latency (ITL) climbs by 5.1× (37.5 ms ➔ 191.0 ms) and TTFT climbs to 1,363 s (22.7 minutes).
    • On H100: Output throughput plateaus at ~59.7 — 61.0 tok/s from c = 8 to c = 512, while TTFT explodes to 4,219 s (70.3 minutes).
  • Why This Baseline Proves the Need for Lustre KV Cache Offloading: At c = 512, maintaining 512 simultaneous 32,768-token prompt KV cache sequences (~5.12 TB) forces physical GPU HBM occupancy to 100% (full token usage = 1.00), triggering severe cache eviction and memory contention. Enabling Lustre KV Cache Offloading will evict inactive 32k KV blocks to high-speed Lustre parallel storage and stream them back over NVLink/RDMA, eliminating HBM saturation and keeping ITL flat at high concurrency.
  • Why This Baseline Proves the Need for LLM-D Disaggregated Prefill-Decode (llm-d-router): In standard co-located serving, incoming 16.7 Million prompt tokens block ongoing decode streams, creating the 22.7-minute (B200) and 70.3-minute (H100) TTFT queueing delays seen at c = 512. Disaggregating prefill nodes from decode nodes (llm-d-router) will isolate the heavy 32k prompt processing, keeping decode ITL around ~20 ms and TTFT under a few seconds!
